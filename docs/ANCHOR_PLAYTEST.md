# ANCHOR comprehension playtest — five minutes

This sheet tests whether the existing loop communicates a learned spatial law
and one deliberate exception. It is not a preference or content test.

## Setup

- Start a fresh run with sound enabled and no prior explanation.
- Leave `F3` diagnostics and `F4` previews off.
- Do not show the participant the technical note or the observer section below.
- If the run is not complete at five minutes, stop and record where it stalled.

## Participant card — show only this

Complete the run by exploring the arena.

```text
WASD        move
Mouse       look
E           interact
R           restart
Esc         release / recapture mouse
```

Think aloud when you notice something change. No puzzle hints will be given.

## Observer record — do not prompt the participant

Record times or short quotes, not interpretations.

| Moment | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| First world change | Time, direction of travel, and what visual evidence they name |
| Movement causality | Whether they test forward/backward motion without prompting |
| ANCHOR discovery | What draws attention and what they think was acquired |
| Eligible target | Whether form/material/reticle is enough to invite an experiment |
| First application | Their prediction before moving through another threshold |
| Resistance | Whether they look at the stable gate and relation echo during/after world motion |
| Fragment stability | Their explanation before collection, if any |
| Exit activation | Whether the center beacon communicates where and why to return |
| Completion or stall | Time, last hypothesis, repeated interactions, and accidental successes |

Suggested pacing for the observer only:

- `0:00–1:15`: establish the normal four-state transformation.
- `1:15–2:15`: discover and interpret the exception.
- `2:15–4:15`: form and test a spatial hypothesis.
- `4:15–5:00`: exploit the relation, return, and complete—or record the stall.

## Post-play questions

Ask these after the run, without correcting the participant between answers.

1. When did you first notice the world changing, and what did you see?
2. What made you think movement did—or did not—cause those changes?
3. Had you learned a predictable normal rule before ANCHOR appeared? Describe it.
4. What did you believe ANCHOR changed immediately after acquiring and applying it?
5. When the world transformed afterward, what did you expect the gate to do, and what did it actually do?
6. Why did the Fragment become reachable, and did you discover that by reasoning or by trying interactions?
7. Did backward movement matter, and what moment looked most like a bug rather than an intentional spatial rule?
